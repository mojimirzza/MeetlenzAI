# Master Decision Report — MeetLens

## Decision
**PRIMARY NEXT MOVE: PILOT / EVIDENCE**

The current repository has enough core functionality to stop broad refactoring. The next highest-leverage activity is to validate the product with real technical/design-review meetings and capture measurable moderator and participant outcomes.

## Why not a major side feature now?
A creative side feature can improve the product, but current evidence is still synthetic. Building another feature before validating the core question-intelligence loop increases product surface area without answering the highest-value question: do real moderators prefer MeetLens' compressed, diverse nominees over manual triage?

## Creative feature reserved for the first post-pilot experiment
**Blind Spot Radar**: a moderator-only, evidence-backed signal showing which participant expertise/perspective areas are underrepresented among submitted questions. It must surface coverage gaps, not invent concerns. It is deliberately deferred until the base pilot establishes a trusted core workflow.

## Primary ICP
Engineering and product leadership running recurring technical architecture/design-review meetings, especially software and fintech teams.

## Pilot experiment
Compare the team's normal/manual question triage with MeetLens in at least three real meetings. Capture:

- raw question count
- distinct intents
- duplicate suppression
- participant coverage
- nominee acceptance
- nominee edit rate
- rejection rate
- time from question intake to moderator decision
- questions actually asked
- answer/usefulness outcome

## Evidence ladder
Current stage:

**E2 — controlled synthetic benchmark / working demo**

Target:

**E3 — controlled external pilot**

The current repository does **not** claim E3.

## Product thesis to prove
MeetLens creates more useful meeting time by reducing semantic redundancy while preserving important minority and expert questions under explicit human control.

## Netherlands relevance
This product has a credible innovation narrative around a new AI-assisted way of organizing collective questions in meetings, but this is not evidence of eligibility or approval. Current IND guidance says the startup route involves, among other things, an innovative business, an active founder role, a reliable facilitator, and a step-by-step plan; RVO advises on innovativeness and the step-by-step plan.
